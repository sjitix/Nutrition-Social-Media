# 2026-10-03 — Track E: the app became operable by hand

**Milestone:** Track E, the 8-hour direct-manipulation day (`docs/v1/05-direct-manipulation.md`).
**Solved:** H1–H8 of 8. The planned scope is complete; the test debt below is owed.

## What moved

Tapping a meal now changes it, and no model is involved in any of it.

- **The spine** (`55b4e50`) — `previewOperations` in the engine, `preview: true` on
  `/api/operation` (checked *before* the executor, so a preview has no path that can commit), the
  allowlist widened by six tools, and `actions.ts` as the single path every control takes.
- **The Meal Sheet** (`2e3294b`) — portion with the clamp disclosed, pin, rate, "why is this here?",
  the recipe, and a chip per ingredient for "no greek yogurt".
- **Swap with the trade shown** (`ee025b0`) — six alternatives per slot, each with the delta it would
  cause, built on the engine's own candidate pool so **a dish the executor would refuse is never
  offered** (0 peanut dishes to a peanut allergy, 0 meat to a vegan, 0 same-day repeats).
- **The macro dial** — name a protein figure, get both routes to it: resize what's there when the
  engine's 1.8× ceiling allows (you keep the meal you were going to cook), else the dishes that do
  reach it.
- **The reconcile sheet** — previews any change: the days it moves, the dish moves, the engine's
  notes, then Apply / Apply + rebalance / Cancel, the rebalance being a separate undoable operation.
- **Drag and drop** — a move is a pair of swaps sent as one call, so it is one undo. Keyboard parity
  is real: `M` lifts a plate, every slot announces "Put X here", and touch gets the same two-tap path
  because HTML5 drag events don't fire on a phone.
- **The deviation flow** — "I ate something else" / "I'm eating out", which re-solves the rest of the
  day. This is the competitive claim and it is measured below.
- **The command palette** (`9ddca92`) — `Ctrl/⌘ K`, a pure parser, plus `u` to undo, and an undo
  toast that reads the engine's undo state rather than assuming a change happened.
- **Fix my week** — one press, every day short of target rebalanced, disclosing that undo reaches
  one day back.

## 17:30 — an adversarial review of D5b, and what it found in the shipped parser

Ultracode was on, so the D5b diff got its own review: four finders (allergen under-blocks, allergen
over-blocks, units, targets and the request day), each finding re-run by a skeptic. Eleven agents,
every finding reproduced end to end. **The allergy parser D5b had just shipped (`889f667`) was worse
than before it for one family of phrasings.** It dropped any clause containing an "allowance", so:

- "I can eat anything without gluten" was served 41 gluten meals in five weeks;
- "Shellfish - everything else is fine" was served shellfish 10 times;
- "Neither dairy nor eggs are ok for me" was served eggs 26 times.

Before D5b, mining the category words caught most of these. Also found:
- "shell fish", "sea food" and "egg's" matched nothing;
- "casein" blocked no dairy;
- the cheese in "cottage cheese" was skipped as a carrier;
- a non-category food ("avocado - life threatening") was never mined;
- over-blocks: "fries" removed 63 stir-fries through the reverse -ies rule, "onions unless cooked"
  added the word "cooked", "spinach (cooked)" and "white or brown rice" left lone adjectives, and
  dislikes got allergy-grade mining ("goat cheese" removed every cheese).

The rework:
1. A clause is dropped only when it plainly allows a SPECIFIC food. It must contain an allowance
   phrase, nothing restrictive, no "everything/anything", and no neither/nor/none anywhere in its
   segment.
2. Text splits into segments (punctuation, " - "), then items, then clauses.
3. Split category names are joined ("shell fish" becomes "shellfish"), and possessives are stripped.
4. Allergies are mined for every curated food a phrase names, dislikes only for the long-standing
   category words.
5. A lone non-food word is never a token.
6. casein, whey, CMPA and tahini are category keys.
7. A gluten allergy no longer removes a dish the library verifies as gluten-free. Fourteen recipes'
   methods said "tortillas" where the ingredients say corn tortillas; they now say so, and two titles
   changed: "Spanish Potato & Pepper Omelette" and "Corn Tortilla & Pepper Scramble".

Units, targets and the request day got the review's fixes too:
- "0,250 l" is no longer read as 250 l;
- "2 tbsp." and "200g/7oz" weigh again;
- a size word before another unit noun, or on an item counted by its parts, is null;
- unambiguous spellings ("tablespoons", "lbs", "cans") resolve;
- a real adult outside the body limits is told the equation is not validated for them, rather than
  that the stat "doesn't look right";
- fluid above BMI 30 is worked from the same reference weight as protein;
- the onboarding message waits until a field is left;
- the request-day window is the real span of the Earth's time zones.

**The fuzzer had the last word.** The first full gate of the rework failed invariant I2: a dislike of
"olive" now kept olive oil (deliberately, since a dislike of olives had removed 203 of 501 recipes),
and the invariant's naive substring oracle flagged it. That raised the real question, allergy versus
dislike. The exception now applies to dislikes only. An ALLERGY to olives or peppers adds "olive oil"
or "black/white pepper" as tokens of their own, so it still over-blocks. The oracle exempts exactly
those compounds, for dislikes only.

**Process faults, both mine, both now prevented (WORKPLAN lesson 62):**
- The D5b ship's rebase reported success, but re-applying its autostash had conflicted. WORKPLAN.md sat
  unmerged with the stash kept, and nothing said so. `ship.mjs` now checks for unmerged paths and a
  leftover autostash after every pull, and exits non-zero naming them.
- I edited `food-units.json` while the next commit's gate was running. The suite reads it at runtime,
  so that gate failed on a change that was not in it.
