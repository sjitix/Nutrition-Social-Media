# 2026-10-21 — models lane: what the prompt quotes, found and held out

**Mandate (owner, unchanged):** the biggest free model with an optimal response time; big models first.
**Solved:** yes, for the key-free plan. Every eval message the prompt quotes now has a held-out paraphrase,
graded by the same code, and a probe keeps it that way. Every model run still waits on a missing `.env.local`.

## What moved

- **`probe-quotes.mjs`: which eval messages the prompt quotes.**
  - **What it reads:** the prompt the model actually gets. loop-eval's bundle prints it with `PRINT_PROMPT`.
  - **What counts as a near-quote**, either test:
    - one prompt line holds at least 75% of a message's content words, and at least three;
    - a user example in the prompt holds three or more words of the message, two of them content words,
      verbatim or with one word of the message left out.
  - **Exit code:** the near-quotes with no held-out, plus any held-out the prompt has come to quote.
  - **Proof it can fail:** `--self-test` plants four lines in the real prompt and requires the count to move
    as it should (4/4).
  - **Before a prompt change lands:** `--code <branch>` or `--prompt-file <draft>`.
  - **On main:** 8 near-quotes in 55 messages.
  - **Three first tries were wrong.**
    - The longest shared run of words missed `contradiction`, which shares only "the whole week" verbatim
      but 4 of its 5 content words.
    - Reading `promptV2.ts` as text counted code comments the model never sees.
    - The first parser skipped four loop scenarios whose message is not on the line after the id
      (`expectRead`, `history`), and one of them, `find-then-place`, was a quote. A skipped scenario now
      stops the probe.
  - **On the wording branch** it finds exactly the two quotes its lines add, "save some room for it" and
    "have it more often". Both were already held out.
- **Four new held-outs, each graded by the same function as its original:**
  - `vague-goal-heldout`: "i want to drop a few pounds, can you help with that?" The original's message
    IS the prompt's CLARIFY example.
  - `emotional-heldout`: "it's that time of the month and i've got zero energy". The original's words are
    in the prompt's list of body states.
  - `every-day-swap-heldout`: "an omelette every morning would make me very happy". The prompt says
    "pancakes every breakfast".
  - `find-then-place-heldout`: "look up a meat-free dinner that's ready in 15 minutes or less and stick it
    on tuesday". The original's requirement is the prompt's own example, "vegetarian, under 20 minutes".
    Two dinners in the library qualify. The 18- and 20-minute ones, which the original allows, are its
    must-fail controls.
  - convo `vegetarian-then-steak`: "i've gone vegetarian, no more meat for me", then "can i get a steak
    for wednesday dinner?". The original's turn 2 echoes the prompt's vegan/chicken example.
  - Each carries controls: its original's, ported, or new ones by hand for `find-then-place` (62 in all).
    The nothing, hollow and garbage fakes score 0 on all five.
- **Found on the way, for v1:** a constrain's cook-time cap gets a 5-minute grace (`maxCookTime + 5` in
  `select.ts` and `execute.ts`), with no note inside it. A day constrain asking for "15 minutes or less"
  placed a 20-minute dinner, and the reply said only "Tuesday now has 2000 kcal and 149g protein".
  `find_recipes`' `maxTime` is strict. Whether an explicit limit should get the grace is v1's call; the
  eval already fails the outcome.
- **Both evals print each held-out beside its original:** `held out x/y, their originals x/y`. They name any
  scenario that passed only in the prompt's words (`summary.heldOut`; each row carries `heldOutOf`).
- **The wording test's `--only` now names its six scenarios.** A bare `heldout` would also run four
  held-outs whose originals are not in that run.
- **586 controls hold across 49 scenarios under 2 engine seeds.**
