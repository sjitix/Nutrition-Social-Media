# 2026-10-30 — D9a hardening: the fuzz sends this week's operations

**Milestone:** V1 D9a (C4), its engine operations under the random-sequence invariants.
**Solved:** in progress. F8, decisions 16 and 17 and D8 still wait on the owner.

## What moved

- **The engine fuzz now sends `move_meal`, `skip_meal`, and one slot under a protein floor or a cook-time limit.**
  None of D9a's operations was in it, so no random sequence had checked them against the invariants: diet,
  allergens, meal count, no repeat in a day, portion bounds, cook time and calories. They are now 17% of the
  operations. 800 sequences, run alone: no violation in the engine. The first runs failed on two faults in the
  test itself:
  - **`calorieReachable` said the opposite of its comment.** For a meal with no base recipe it returned
    "reachable" under the comment "can't judge; don't flag". A skipped lunch is held at 0 kcal, so a day whose
    other two meals were already at the 1.8x ceiling was flagged at 1699 kcal. Such a meal now counts at its own
    calories, as the engine holds it.
  - **A one-slot re-plan that changed nothing ended a day's exemption.** A day made bigger on purpose is
    exempt from the calorie check. "Already has 45g protein" rightly leaves that day as it was, yet the bookkeeping
    cleared the exemption on any `regenerate_day`. Only a change to the day clears it now.
  - `FUZZ_ALL=1` prints every violation rather than the first of each kind.

- **B6, on the ask path: a remembered health condition earns an offer, never a change (D8's parallel item).**
  "I'm pregnant" was remembered, then changed nothing and said nothing about food. The condition-aware selector
  had been built and tested since September but never wired: VISION says ask before adjusting, and the open
  question was how. Now the engine itself writes the offer when a remembered fact makes a nutrient newly called
  for. It names the condition and its nutrients and offers to favour the first one the library can supply under
  the person's diet, with the doctor line. Nothing changes until a yes, which the model sends as
  `constrain(boostNutrient)`. Because the offer is an engine note, it is shown as written, so a model can neither
  skip the question nor claim it already adjusted.
  - No offer for a fact restated, a preference, a denied deficiency ("not deficient in iron"), or a turn that
    already boosts.
  - The prompt now says: remember a condition and change nothing; on a yes, boost.
  - New public names: `conditionOffer` (plan/report) and `liveConditions` (data/conditions). 5 rows.
  - Auto-applying a durable condition to a fresh first week stays out: it needs a place to disclose it, and
    `WeekPlan` has no notes field.

- **96 recipes, 618 to 714, where the library was thinnest.** Measured first. Vegetarian had 66 lunches and 67
  dinners against 138 breakfasts. Vegan had 43 lunches and 49 dinners, keto 31 and 41. Pork (11 dishes) and beef
  (25) were the scarcest proteins. The owner's target is 900.
  - Batch one (`31af5d6`): 8 vegetarian lunches, 8 vegetarian dinners, 4 pork and 4 beef.
  - Batch two (`4b67ae2`): 8 vegan lunches, 8 vegan dinners, 4 keto lunches and 4 keto dinners, across cuisines
    rather than more Asian tofu bowls.
  - Batch three (`edf9cfb`): 8 keto breakfasts (32 before), 8 Mediterranean lunches and 8 dinners (49 and 57).
  - Batch four: breakfasts for the thinnest cuisines (Indian 16, Italian 17, Mexican and Asian 18, against
    American's 34), 4 each, and 8 keto snacks (33 before).
  - All curated ingredients; every one through `check:drafts` first.
  - **Batch one's gate failed twice, and both were real defects the new dishes exposed:**
    - **"Greek salad" under a dairy allergy stopped saying why.** The lookup that explains a refusal takes the best
      keyword match with the allergies cleared, and it scores ingredients and descriptions too. The new Pork
      Souvlaki plate (Greek yogurt, "a tomato salad") won the tie-break over Chicken Souvlaki with Greek Salad.
      Its name holds neither word, so the explanation was dropped. A dish carrying every asked word in its name is
      now looked for first.
    - **A 5-star dish could be traded away.** The selector places a loved dish on purpose, but the protein
      rebalance then replaced it like any other meal: Beef Stir-Fry Noodles was missing from 9 of 200 weeks. A dish
      rated 4 or 5 is now never the one replaced. 0 of 200 after.
  - **`tsc` caught `mainProtein: "tempeh"`**, which is not a value (tempeh dishes are "tofu"), only after the
    drafts were pasted. `check:drafts` now checks cuisine and mainProtein too.

- **An independent culinary review of the 96 new recipes** found 12 problems in 11 of them:
  - **Three pasta dinners never cooked their pasta** (the puttanescas, the lentil ragu).
  - **A turkey snack rolled up RAW turkey breast.**
  - **Two diet tags an ingredient can make false.** Kimchi is often made with fish sauce, and many burger buns
    contain milk or egg. Each step now names the version to buy, as the library's other kimchi dishes already do.
  - Smaller ones: a "slaw" with no dressing, spinach said to be wilted that nothing wilted, "masala" with turmeric
    alone (cumin added), half a lemon never used, corn tortillas rolled cold, and "toasted" nuts nothing toasts.
- **The raw turkey went further than the snack.** `turkey-breast` is USDA's raw breast, and 9 older recipes
  layered or rolled it as if it were sliced cooked turkey: wraps, roll-ups, salads. Each now pan-cooks it first,
  12 minutes longer. A cooked deli-turkey ingredient would be the better shape; it is a new ingredient, so it
  belongs with decision 17.

- **Batch five, 714 to 737 (`0dd8e0e`):** 8 vegan breakfasts (44 before), 8 snacks for the four cuisines with
  the fewest (two each, none a near-copy of an existing one), and 7 keto lunches. `check:drafts` caught two names
  the library has, four lunches under the floor, and kimchi on a gluten-free dish. The prawn cocktail folded in
  "the cooked prawns" while `prawns` is USDA raw shrimp, so it now cooks them.
  - **Its first gate failed the fuzz's I5 once.** A day the person had made bigger took a one-meal protein floor,
    and that floor keeps the other portions when rebalancing would lower protein. The fuzz ended the day's
    exemption on any one-slot re-plan; now only a whole-day one does. 800 sequences: no ordinary day left its
    target.
- **An audit for ingredients no step cooks, across all 737 recipes (`f1da262`'s raw turkey, generalised).**
  - **39 clear cases.** 35 never cooked their grain, pasta or noodles: "serve over rice" when the rice is
    "Rice, brown, long-grain, raw" (16), plus quinoa, bulgur, couscous, pasta and noodles. Stuffed peppers baked
    dry rice with no liquid. 4 served raw chicken or egg: a pesto chicken penne tossed in chicken nothing cooked.
  - **Also:** 11 snacks said "boiled eggs" with nothing that boils them, an omelette method ran backwards, and
    the salmon poke bowl, raw by design, now names sushi-grade salmon frozen first, or searing it.
  - **52 recipes fixed**, each with one step in the library's own words; times rise where cooking adds time.
  - **`check:drafts` now warns on it,** and `-- --library` runs the rule over every recipe. On the library as it
    stood before the fixes it flagged 30 of the 39, plus the poke bowl and the omelette. It misses a verb that
    belongs to something else in the same step. After the fixes: 0 of 737.
