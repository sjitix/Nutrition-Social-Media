## What moved

- **An adversarial review of the batch (C, D, E, F) and of B2, which had landed** (four reviewers, then a
  skeptic per serious finding). 25 findings, 14 serious. All six that went to a skeptic were confirmed,
  and no allergen reached a plate in any of their runs. Every serious one is fixed in the batch:
  - **C's note named a replacement that wasn't there.** A rebalance or a pin changed the slot after
    selection: stale in 10 of 120 seeded runs, and in every pinned slot. The note now names the dish
    and the rule, not the replacement (a pin back in the slot is named).
  - **Imports and the other hard rules.** A new vegetarian diet or a new exclusion kept an import that
    broke it, silently. An import now answers to a diet or a food avoided when THAT change introduced
    it. One placed over an old rule stays, because it was put there on purpose. Allergies always count.
    An import titled like a library dish is judged by its own ingredients.
  - **D's fallback could show a line written before the engine ran.** "I've made Tuesday vegetarian"
    stood over a budget change. Only a memory-only step's line survives to an empty finishing turn now.
  - **The undo slot.** A write that changed nothing still took it, and an undo inside a run reverted
    the previous turn too. Both are fixed: "changed" is compared, not flagged, and an in-run undo
    reverts the run's own change and drops the notes of the steps it reverted.
  - **E's notes:** "less than before" fired at 0 → 0, and "new rules" was said when none changed.
  - **B2's claim guard replaced three real honest replies** (a refusal, a decline, a question that
    offered a change), and it missed two real claims. The guard now judges a promise only in a reply
    that asks nothing, among other narrowings.
  - **B2's refused-dish note blamed an allergy for a word tie.** "veggie burger" read "Cheeseburger &
    Fries contains dairy" in 45 of 92 such notes. A refused dish is named now only when it matches
    every word asked; a dish the diet refuses says so.
  - **B2's "nothing with it is planned"** was false while an import still held the allergy. Every
    allergy sentence is now written from the turn's final week, and a restated or denied bound allergy
    that a planned dish still holds re-solves the week.

## What got in the way

- **The machine is loaded** (three sessions, the accounts lane's WSL live runs), so a test:engine gate
  takes over an hour. That is why C, D, E, F, G and the B2 follow-ups go out as one commit.
- **The gate ran twice.** It started at 00:52, and the laptop slept with it running: in seven and a half hours its
  test process used 146 seconds of CPU. Three minutes after the machine woke, at 08:29, every session's processes
  were killed a third of the way through the gate, the second review's with them, and the sessions came back at
  15:15. Nothing was committed, and the worktree held the batch intact, so it was simply started again at 15:17.
  - **The killed ship reported exit code 4.** In ship's own codes, 4 means "pushed, but the folder could not catch
    up". But its log ends mid-test with none of the `ship:` lines every one of its own exits prints, and origin had
    no such commit. The 4 came from whatever killed it.
  - **A background gate lives only as long as its session.** After a restart, read the end of the ship's log and
    `git log origin/main` before believing anything landed, or failed.
- **A docs ship collided with another lane's gate.** This lane's `0950eb7` touched CONTEXT.md and the
  worklog index while the accounts lane's gated ship held them. Ship v2 stopped it as designed (exit 2),
  so nothing was lost, but an hour of gate was. The rule already exists (README §4: shared docs ship on
  their own, with --no-gate, never inside a gated ship); the accounts lane now splits its ships that way.
